Here's what to do, in order.

1. Stop auditing after one more test. The audit has done its job. Every additional check will find another confound, because there are always more confounds — that's true of every paper ever published. You now have a claim that has survived magnitude, gene density, cell state, statistical power, and chromosome. That's more control than most published work carries. One more (core-promoter sequence classes, for the independent positive control) and then you write.

2. Run the core-promoter test. TATA / Inr / DPE by JASPAR scan on hg38. Sequence-defined, chromatin-independent, answers the housekeeping question in the Haberle–Stark framing, and gives you a second independent line. Check chr19 share first, as cluster Claude says.

3. Then Phase 0 corrections, figures, write. No new questions.

One thing to check about chr19 before treating it only as a confound. chr19 is the most gene-dense chromosome in the genome. So "chr19 has distinctive contact architecture" may be gene density wearing a chromosome label — and density is already in your control set. Run chr19 displacement with density controlled. Two outcomes, both useful:

Survives density control → chromosome-level architecture is a real, unreported phenomenon and belongs in the report as a finding, not just a nuisance. Nobody has looked at this at bp resolution.
Collapses → it's density again, you already control for it, and the Lambert demotion partly reverses.

Either way it takes an hour and it's the difference between "we found another confound" and "we found something."
