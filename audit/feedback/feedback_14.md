The literature pull turned up one thing that changes how the headline must be written, and two warnings.

The complication. Sexton et al. (2024) clustered a chromatin-state-by-contact matrix into 18 Chromatin Interaction Signatures and reported super-enhancers significantly enriched in specific enhancer-contacting clusters. That is a published positive claim in exactly our territory, and it has to be engaged with rather than ignored.

The reconciliation is our own framing: enrichment is not separation. A set can be reliably displaced toward a region while overlapping almost completely with the rest — which is precisely what our SE set does (|d| 0.16–0.24, 94% overlap). Sexton would call that an enrichment; we call it a non-separation. Both are true, and saying so is far stronger than pretending there is no disagreement. Their input is also chromatin-state-derived, which is the same "chromatin predicts chromatin" concern that demoted three of our own controls.

The reassurance. The continuum position is well precedented, not heterodox. Crowley et al. (2026) find low-dimensional polytopes rather than clusters in Tabula Sapiens; Cano-Gamez et al. (2020) describe an "effectorness gradient" in CD4+ T cells specifically; Shoval/Hart give the Pareto-front formalism where vertices are specialists and the interior is a continuum. Frame the result as applying that reasoning to contact architecture, not as an isolated negative.

A methods contribution I had not recognised. The field's standard controls are O/E normalisation, ligation bias, positional heterogeneity and depth. Gene length, expression, CpG, density and chromosome are not standard — and controlling them overturned two of our claims. That is worth stating in its own right.
